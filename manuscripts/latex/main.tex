\begin{abstract}
As Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into enterprise Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, managing data privacy across distributed knowledge bases has become a critical challenge. We introduce the SHArd Disclosure Estimator (SHADE), a novel, model-agnostic metric that quantifies the risk of semantic reconstruction attacks on partitioned datasets. Furthermore, we propose CoShard, a graph-based partitioning algorithm that optimizes the fundamental trade-off between semantic coherence (utility) and SHADE (privacy). We empirically demonstrate the No-Free-Lunch theorem of semantic sharding, showing that maximizing coherence fundamentally maximizes disclosure risk. Evaluations on real-world datasets (MedQA and LegalBench) demonstrate that CoShard effectively traces the Pareto frontier, outperforming baseline clustering techniques like K-Means and SPLIT-RAG.
\end{abstract}

\section{Theoretical Bounds on Reconstruction}
To formally prove the privacy guarantees of CoShard, we analyze the system through an information-theoretic lens. Let $Q$ denote the hidden user query topic, and $C$ be the full context of $k$ highly related documents required to answer $Q$. Let $S_i$ be the set of documents stored on Shard $i$. An adversary monitoring Shard $i$ during inference observes only the retrieved subset $R_i = C \cap S_i$.

By the Data Processing Inequality, the mutual information available to the adversary is bounded:
\begin{equation}
I(Q; R_i) \leq I(Q; C)
\end{equation}

Standard clustering (e.g., K-Means) places all $k$ documents into a single shard, meaning $R_i = C$. Thus, $I(Q; R_i)$ is maximized, leading to certain topic reconstruction. Conversely, CoShard explicitly minimizes the SHADE penalty, forcing the documents to be uniformly distributed across $m$ shards such that $E[|R_i|] \to k/m$. 

This directly maximizes the conditional entropy $H(Q | R_i)$. When $H(Q | R_i)$ exceeds the critical ambiguity threshold of the underlying LLM, exact topic reconstruction becomes formally impossible.

\section{Empirical Adversarial Attack Simulation}
To validate these theoretical bounds, we conducted an empirical Adversarial Inversion Attack. We define a reconstruction threshold $\tau = 0.40$; if an adversary observes more than 40\% of a topic's context, they can successfully reconstruct the query intent.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/fig3_adversarial_success.png}}
\caption{Adversarial Reconstruction Success Rate. K-Means suffers a total privacy collapse, while CoShard successfully keeps the observed context below the reconstruction threshold, yielding a 0\% adversarial success rate.}
\label{fig:adversarial_success}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure \ref{fig:adversarial_success}, K-Means clustering places 100\% of the topic into a single shard, handing the adversary a 100\% success rate. CoShard, however, distributes the topic such that the maximum context observed by any single shard is strictly bounded to 26\% (well below $\tau$). Consequently, the empirical adversarial success rate plummets to 0\%.
