\section{Interpretive Risk Model and Topological Exposure}
\label{sec:privacy_analysis}

While the SHADE metric serves as a practical heuristic for graph partitioning, it is helpful to contextualize the disclosure risk within an interpretive mathematical framework. In this section, we provide an information-theoretic intuition for topological risk reduction, followed by an interpretive risk model simulation. We explicitly emphasize that CoShard does not provide cryptographic or differential privacy guarantees; rather, it provides a software-layer defense-in-depth by reducing single-shard topological exposure.

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Intuition}
We analyze the semantic reconstruction threat model through an information-theoretic lens. 

Let $Q$ denote a random variable representing the hidden, highly sensitive user query intent. Let $C = \{d_1, \dots, d_k\}$ be the full, minimal context of $k$ highly related corporate documents required by the LLM to successfully synthesize a response to $Q$. The joint information contained in $C$ fully specifies the underlying semantic topic $T$.

Let the vector space be partitioned into $m$ isolated shards $\mathcal{P} = \{S_1, \dots, S_m\}$. Assume an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ compromises Shard $i$. The adversary observes only the local subset of documents $R_i = C \cap S_i$. The mutual information $I(Q ; R_i)$ quantifies the reduction in the adversary's uncertainty about the query intent $Q$ after observing the compromised shard's response $R_i$. 

By the fundamental Data Processing Inequality (DPI), the flow of information forms a Markov chain $Q \rightarrow C \rightarrow R_i$. Consequently, the mutual information available to the adversary is bounded by the total information in the full context:
\begin{equation}
I(Q; R_i) \leq I(Q; C)
\end{equation}

In standard utility-maximizing clustering frameworks like K-Means or Leiden, the algorithm actively minimizes cross-shard edges. It tends to place all $k$ related documents into a single shard, meaning $R_i \approx C$. In this failure state, $I(Q; R_i)$ approaches its upper bound.

Conversely, CoShard acts as a dispersive force on related documents. We formalize this intuition:

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (Topological Exposure Bound):} \textit{If topic documents are distributed across shards such that the maximum concentration on any single shard is bounded by a fraction $\rho \in (0, 1)$, then the adversary observes at most $\rho$ of the topic context. Consequently, the hypothetical reconstruction probability under a threshold-based generative model is bounded by a function of $\rho$.}

By actively starving the adversary of semantic context (enforcing a low $\rho$), CoShard directly maximizes the conditional entropy $H(Q | R_i)$, mitigating semantic disclosure exposure.

\subsection{Interpretive Risk Model Simulation}
To illustrate this proposition intuitively before moving to our empirical attacks in Section \ref{sec:experiments}, we construct a synthetic risk simulation. Drawing from logistic growth models, we model a hypothetical LLM's guessing probability $P(\text{Success})$ using a synthetic sigmoid function:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{Success}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\alpha(x - \tau)}}
\end{equation}
where $x$ is the fraction of context observed ($|R_i| / |C|$), $\tau = 0.40$ is an interpretive threshold (the minimum context required to hallucinate correctly), and $\alpha$ is the steepness parameter.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig3_adversarial_success.png}}
\caption{Interpretive Reconstruction Success Probability. High-utility frameworks yield high context concentration, resulting in near 100\% simulated vulnerability. CoShard drops the simulated success probability significantly.}
\label{fig:adversarial_success}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure \ref{fig:adversarial_success}, standard Leiden clustering hands the adversary nearly the entire topic, driving $x \to 1.0$. CoShard distributes the topic graph smoothly across the network, dropping the theoretical simulated success probability to a mere 6\%. This interpretive framework provides the mathematical intuition for the empirical generative extraction attacks executed in Section \ref{sec:empirical_attack}.
