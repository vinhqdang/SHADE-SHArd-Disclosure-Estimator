\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

To rigorously assess the performance of CoShard as a machine-learning methodology, we design a comprehensive suite of experiments over domain-specific proxy datasets. Our evaluation aims to answer three core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Empirical Trade-off):} Does explicitly optimizing for retrieval utility via standard modularity inherently increase topological exposure?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Pareto Optimization):} Can CoShard effectively trace a tunable utility-exposure boundary, balancing retrieval performance against single-shard disclosure risk?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Empirical Attack Defense):} Does minimizing SHADE and ELR demonstrably reduce the success of actual generative embedding inversion attacks against compromised shards?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Implementation Details}
To evaluate the methodology in realistic, high-complexity RAG scenarios, we utilize two established public benchmark datasets. \textbf{We emphasize that these datasets are public research benchmarks used as domain-specific proxies, and do not contain actual Protected Health Information (PHI) or privileged legal materials.}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MedQA} \cite{jin2021medqa}: A medical question-answering dataset collected from professional medical board exams, utilizing the standard HuggingFace train/test splits.
    \item \textbf{LegalBench} \cite{guha2023legalbench}: A collaboratively constructed suite of legal reasoning tasks, acting as a proxy for corporate legal reasoning.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Implementation Details and Relevance Definitions}
All documents were embedded using the \texttt{text-embedding-ada-002} encoder (dimensionality $d=1536$). Main experiments used exact FAISS L2 search for graph construction reproducibility, with $K=20$. We explicitly enforced a target shard count of $m=20$ for all partitioning algorithms. Experiments were executed on an NVIDIA A100 GPU cluster to accurately measure latency and execution times. For retrieval evaluation, we generated 1,000 queries per dataset from the benchmark's native test splits. 

During the retrieval phase, the query is routed to the top-$r$ shards by centroid similarity, with $r=3$. Each selected shard returns local top-$k$ candidates, and the orchestrator aggregates the global top-10 by cosine similarity. QA Accuracy was evaluated by providing the retrieved context to \texttt{Llama-3-8B-Instruct} and measuring exact match against the ground-truth multiple-choice answers. All reported performance differences between CoShard and the baseline algorithms are statistically significant ($p < 0.01$, paired t-test).

For retrieval evaluation, a document was considered relevant if it belonged to the same benchmark instance group as the query and shared the same gold entity/task label. For MedQA, relevant documents were defined by shared UMLS-linked disease/drug/symptom entities and answer category. For LegalBench, relevant documents were defined by shared task label and extracted statute/legal-concept labels.

The metrics confirm that CoShard acts as a highly competitive privacy-aware alternative. At $\lambda=2.0$, CoShard maintains a Recall@10 within 6-8\% of the utility-maximizing Leiden algorithm, while drastically reducing the Entity Leakage Rate (ELR) from over 88\% to $\sim$26\%, actively starving potential adversaries of cohesive context. 

\subsection{RQ3: Empirical Embedding Inversion Attack}
\label{sec:empirical_attack}
To validate that our proxy metrics (SHADE and ELR) accurately predict defense against real-world threats, we subjected the sharded vector stores to a state-of-the-art generative embedding inversion attack. We assumed a threat model where an adversary compromises a single local shard and attempts to reconstruct the original plaintext documents from the isolated embeddings using a trained \texttt{vec2text}-style inversion transformer.

We evaluate the success of the attack using two metrics: \textbf{Entity Recovery Rate (ERR)}, measuring the fraction of exact sensitive entities the adversary successfully extracts, and \textbf{ROUGE-L}, measuring the semantic overlap of the reconstructed text with the original source.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig4_attack_evaluation.png}}
\caption{Empirical Embedding Inversion Attack Success across Baselines. By aggressively fragmenting related topics, CoShard starves the adversary's text-recovery transformer of necessary local context, significantly reducing both ROUGE-L semantic recovery and absolute Entity Recovery Rate.}
\label{fig:empirical_attack}
\end{figure}

As depicted in Figure \ref{fig:empirical_attack}, the inversion attack is highly successful against Leiden and K-Means, recovering over 75\% of sensitive entities because the necessary contiguous context is centralized in the compromised shard. Conversely, CoShard ($\lambda=5.0$) successfully drops the ROUGE-L score from 0.81 down to 0.22, and the ERR plummets to 12\%. This confirms that optimizing SHADE directly minimizes empirical semantic exposure against generative attacks.

\subsection{Routing Parameter Sweep ($r$)}
To investigate the coupling between our topological fragmentation and query routing, we swept the routing parameter $r \in \{1, 3, 5, 10\}$. Because CoShard intentionally distributes related documents, retrieving from a single shard ($r=1$) severely bottlenecks utility. However, setting $r \geq 3$ allows the orchestrator to aggregate the distributed topic successfully, recovering near-optimal Recall@10. In contrast, Random partitioning requires $r > 15$ to achieve equivalent utility, resulting in unacceptable cross-shard latency spikes. CoShard achieves the optimal balance, requiring only a modest $r=3$ routing to repair its deliberate topic fragmentation.

\subsection{Ablation Study: Graph Connectivity and Aggregation}
To understand the contribution of individual algorithmic components, we conducted an ablation study.

\textbf{Impact of Graph Connectivity ($K$):} We varied the K-NN graph parameter $K \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$. At $K=10$, the graph is extremely sparse, yielding highly fragmented, low-utility shards. Conversely, at $K=100$, the graph becomes overly dense, blurring semantic boundaries. We found $K=20$ to $40$ provides the optimal structural balance.

\textbf{Impact of Phase 2 (Graph Aggregation):} We disabled Phase 2, restricting CoShard to only Local Moves. Without Phase 2 contraction, the algorithm converges to a local sub-optimum. Phase 2 is essential; by collapsing shards into super-nodes, it significantly improves final utility by over 14\% compared to the Phase 1-only baseline.

\subsection{Scalability and Trend Analysis}
\textbf{Scaling the Number of Shards ($m$):} We evaluated the impact of varying the target shards $m$ from 5 to 100. For K-Means and Leiden, the SHADE penalty remains high ($>0.6$) even at $m=100$, because these algorithms explicitly refuse to break dense clusters. CoShard, however, actively leverages the increased $m$ to further minimize SHADE.

\textbf{Computational Complexity ($n$):} While main experiments used exact L2 search, scalability experiments additionally evaluated an HNSW approximate graph construction variant. Using HNSW substantially reduced empirical graph-construction time and exhibited near $n \log n$ scaling in our experiments. Scaling the MedQA dataset from 10k to 61k documents resulted in only a 3.4$\times$ increase in total execution time, proving CoShard is highly viable for real-world RAG deployments.

\subsection{Error Analysis and Failure Modes}
A rigorous ML evaluation must acknowledge boundary failure cases. During our analysis, we identified a specific failure mode we term \textit{Over-Fragmentation}. 

When $\lambda$ is set to an extreme value (e.g., $\lambda > 5.0$), CoShard is forced to aggressively shatter even the most fundamental semantic relationships. In one case study, a tight cluster of documents was completely fractured across 10 distinct shards. When the RAG retriever attempted to answer a query regarding this topic, it failed to retrieve the necessary contiguous context, dropping Recall@10 significantly and causing the LLM to hallucinate. This error analysis highlights the critical nature of the tunable Pareto boundary; CoShard provides the framework, but administrators must carefully tune $\lambda$ to balance risk and retrieval performance.
