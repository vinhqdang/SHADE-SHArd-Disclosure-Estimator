\section{Introduction}
The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) over private enterprise data has unlocked unprecedented utility through Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). However, hosting centralized vector databases containing sensitive corporate or medical knowledge poses significant privacy and compliance risks. To mitigate these risks, organizations increasingly rely on distributed architectures, partitioning data across multiple isolated shards. 

While existing partitioning techniques such as K-Means or Leiden clustering excel at maintaining semantic coherence (utility), they inadvertently cluster highly related sensitive documents onto the same shard. This creates a critical vulnerability: if an adversary compromises a single shard or the local LLM instance querying it, they can reconstruct the entire underlying sensitive topic. 

In this paper, we address this critical gap by introducing the \textbf{SHArd Disclosure Estimator (SHADE)}, a novel metric that mathematically quantifies the risk of semantic reconstruction attacks. We subsequently propose \textbf{CoShard}, a graph-based partitioning algorithm that explicitly optimizes the trade-off between RAG utility and SHADE privacy, allowing administrators to seamlessly trace the Pareto optimal frontier of semantic sharding.
