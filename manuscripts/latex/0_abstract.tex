As large language models are increasingly deployed in retrieval-augmented generation systems, partitioning sensitive corpora across distributed vector stores has become an important design problem. We introduce SHADE, a graph-based proxy metric for estimating semantic co-location risk in sharded embedding spaces, and CoShard, a privacy-aware graph partitioning algorithm that trades off retrieval utility against single-shard disclosure risk. CoShard augments modularity-based partitioning with a disclosure penalty that discourages highly related documents from being concentrated in a single shard. Experiments on MedQA and LegalBench show that standard coherence-maximizing partitioning methods increase single-shard semantic concentration, whereas CoShard provides a tunable privacy-utility trade-off. Our results suggest that topology-aware sharding can reduce reconstruction risk while preserving retrieval performance, providing a practical direction for privacy-aware RAG deployment.
